Planning by Time Horizon

The timeframe of your objective dictates where capital should reside. Capital earmarked for near-term use requires stability, while multi-year horizons allow equity compounding.

0 to 2 Years

Short-Term Reserves

Prioritizes capital preservation, rapid liquidity, and inflation protection for immediate living requirements.

  • 3–6 Month Emergency Fund: Protection against income disruptions or emergencies.
  • Relocation or Wedding: Near-term expenses with inflexible dates.
  • Annual Property Tax Reserves: Predictable recurring obligations.
3 to 7 Years

Intermediate Milestones

Balances conservative growth with measured downside mitigation for major life transactions.

  • Home Purchase Down Payment: Target reserves for conventional mortgage down payments.
  • Business Launch Seed Fund: Starting capital or equity contribution for private ventures.
  • Secondary Vehicle Acquisition: Cash allocation to minimize borrowing interest.
8+ Years

Long-Term Wealth

Focuses on maximum real purchasing power growth through diversified equity and tax-advantaged accounts.

  • Retirement Independence: Building sustainable perpetual withdrawal capital.
  • Higher Education Funding (529): Multi-year tax-advantaged college savings.
  • Intergenerational Wealth: Estate preservation and trust account planning.

The Mazukarin Planning Protocol

A step-by-step method to organize monthly cash flow and direct every dollar toward a defined priority.

STEP 01

Audit & Establish Baseline

Inventory after-tax net income, mandatory overhead, and non-discretionary costs. Target an allocation where fixed expenses do not exceed 50% of monthly net income.

STEP 02

Eliminate Toxic Liabilities

Aggressively pay down non-collateralized consumer credit and debts carrying APRs above 8% before funding market-facing goals, securing an immediate guaranteed return.

STEP 03

Account-Goal Pairing

Assign each milestone to the correct vehicle—such as High-Yield Accounts for liquidity, Brokerage for intermediate flexibility, and IRAs for retirement.

STEP 04

Automate Inflows

Establish split direct deposits from payroll to route funds immediately into goal silos on payday, preventing lifestyle creep and missed contributions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions regarding goal prioritization, emergency buffers, and debt balance.

Should I fund my emergency buffer or invest for retirement first?

Prioritize capturing any employer retirement match first (which represents an immediate 100% return on eligible contributions). Concurrently, accumulate at least one month of bare-bones living expenses in cash before expanding investments.

How large should my emergency reserve actually be?

Single-earner households, freelancers, and commission-based professionals should maintain 6 months of baseline living expenses. Dual-earner households with stable corporate salaries generally operate efficiently with 3 to 4 months of expenses.

How do I balance saving for a down payment while investing for retirement?

Maintain baseline retirement contributions (typically 10–15% of gross income) rather than stopping them completely. Direct any surplus above that baseline toward intermediate high-yield and low-volatility fixed-income accounts dedicated to the home fund.

What is the recommended approach to multiple simultaneous goals?

Use a waterfall prioritization model. Allocate 70% of available monthly surplus to your top primary milestone (e.g., house down payment) and divide the remaining 30% across secondary items to maintain progress across all fronts.
